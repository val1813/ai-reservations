# Reviewer 2 (Condensed Matter Experiment) — Report

**Manuscript:** PRD Letter — "Correlation Quadrature Uncertainty as a Falsification Criterion for Standard Quantum Mechanics"

**Reviewer background:** Experimental condensed matter physics (fractional quantum Hall, cold atoms, superconducting qubits). I evaluate the experimental claims from the perspective of someone who has spent years in the lab trying to actually measure things.

---

## Overall Recommendation: **REJECT**

**Primary experimental feasibility reason for rejection:** None of the three proposed experimental systems has a concrete, step-by-step measurement protocol that would yield the claimed observables. The paper asserts measurability without demonstrating it. For a paper whose central contribution is an "experimentally falsifiable" inequality, this is a fatal defect.

---

## Finding 1: FQH Interferometry -- Measurement Protocol Is Nonsense [FATAL]

### The Paper's Claim

The paper claims (Section V, System 1) that Fabry-Perot interferometry at filling fraction $\nu = 1/3$ can be used to extract $C^R_{mn}$ and $C^I_{mn}$, and that Werkmeister et al. (Science 388, 730, 2025) provides "the same infrastructure."

### What Is Actually Measured

Werkmeister et al. measure the **anyon braiding phase** $\theta_a = 2\pi\nu$ through interference of edge modes traversing the interferometer. The key observable is the interference contribution to the conductance:

$$G \propto \text{Re}\left[t_L t_R^* \langle e^{i(\phi_L - \phi_R)}\rangle\right]$$

This gives you the **complex transmission amplitude** of the entire interferometer -- a single complex number that encodes the braiding statistics of the enclosed quasiparticles. It does NOT give you the element-wise correlation matrix $C_{mn} = \langle c^\dagger_n c_m \rangle$ between arbitrary pairs of edge sites.

### The Gap

To extract $C^R_{mn}$ and $C^I_{mn}$ from an FQH interferometer, one would need:

1. **Spatial resolution at the level of individual edge channels.** The Fabry-Perot interferometer couples to the total edge current, not to specific pairs of sites $m,n$. The paper uses a lattice fermion notation ($c^\dagger_n c_m$), but FQH edges are chiral Luttinger liquids where the fundamental fields are bosonic $\phi(x)$, not lattice fermions. The relationship between $\langle c^\dagger_n c_m \rangle$ in a discrete lattice model and the continuum edge correlator $\langle e^{i\sqrt{3}\phi(x)} e^{-i\sqrt{3}\phi(y)}\rangle$ is never established.

2. **Independent control over individual edge segments.** To measure $C_{mn}$ for a specific pair $(m,n)$, you need to address those two sites independently. The Fabry-Perot geometry has exactly two quantum point contacts (QPCs). That gives you access to two tunneling points. To reconstruct the full $L \times L$ correlation matrix, you would need $O(L^2)$ independently addressable probes. This is not physically possible in the proposed geometry.

3. **A reconstruction protocol for $C^R$ and $C^I$ from interferometric visibility and phase.** Even if one measures the visibility $\mathcal{V}$ and phase shift $\Delta\phi$ of the interference pattern as a function of magnetic field and gate voltage, this yields at best $\sim 2$ degrees of freedom (magnitude and phase of the interference term), not the $L(L-1)/2$ independent elements of the correlation matrix.

### The Deeper Problem

The paper acknowledges that "the precise functional form of $\Gamma(\nu)$ requires a microscopic Chern-Simons derivation beyond this Letter's scope." But this concedes the entire point: **without knowing $\Gamma(\nu)$, you cannot compute the predicted bound violation magnitude, and therefore cannot tell an experimentalist what signal to look for.**

The claim that the "qualitative signature ($\Delta n$ non-monotonicity)" can guide experimental search is hand-waving at the level of "look for something weird." No experimentalist can write a proposal based on "non-monotonicity."

### Verdict on System 1

**The FQH experimental proposal is not merely underdeveloped -- it is based on a category error.** The Werkmeister measurement and the proposed correlation matrix measurement probe completely different physical observables. The relationship between them is never derived. Until the paper provides: (a) a mapping between the lattice observables $C_{mn}$ and the continuum edge fields, (b) a concrete measurement protocol with a specific interferometer geometry and gate configuration, and (c) a quantitative prediction for the expected violation magnitude at $\nu = 1/3$ with realistic parameters, System 1 must be considered pure speculation.

## Finding 2: 4-Qubit Tomography -- Coherence Time and SNR [FATAL]

### The Paper's Claim

The paper proposes (Section V, System 2) "superconducting qubit chains (4-8 transmons) with individual dispersive readout" to measure $\kappa_{ij}$, the connected four-point cumulant that characterizes Wick breakdown.

### The Scale of the Problem

Full quantum state tomography of $N = 4$ qubits requires $4^N = 256$ Pauli basis measurements. For each measurement setting, the dispersive readout is projective and returns a binary string (e.g., $|0101\rangle$). To estimate the density matrix element $\rho_{ij}$ to precision $\epsilon$, the number of repetitions per measurement setting is:

$$N_{\text{reps}} \approx \frac{1}{\epsilon^2 \cdot \text{SNR}_{\text{single}}^2}$$

Now, the paper needs to measure the **connected** four-point cumulant $\kappa_{ij}$ to $\sim 10\%$ precision (since the claimed violation is 10-30%). The connected cumulant is the difference between four-point and two-point correlations:

$$\kappa_{ij} = \langle n_i n_j\rangle - \langle n_i\rangle\langle n_j\rangle + |C_{ij}|^2$$

This is a **small difference of large numbers.** If $\langle n_i n_j\rangle \approx 0.25$, $\langle n_i\rangle\langle n_j\rangle \approx 0.25$, and $|C_{ij}|^2 \approx 0.01$, then $\kappa_{ij} \approx 0.01$ with an error bar dominated by the individual terms. To resolve $\kappa_{ij}$ at 10%, you need to know the individual two-point functions to $\sim 10^{-3}$ absolute precision. That requires:

$$N_{\text{reps}} \approx 10^6 \text{ per Pauli setting}$$

For 256 settings, that is $2.56 \times 10^8$ total measurements. At a typical dispersive readout time of $\sim 500$ ns per shot (IBM-style transmons), this requires:

$$T_{\text{total}} \approx 2.56 \times 10^8 \times 500 \text{ ns} \approx 128 \text{ seconds}$$

### The Coherence Problem

The longest $T_2$ times in state-of-the-art transmon qubits are $\sim 500$ $\mu$s (and that's for single qubits, not coupled chains under strong driving). You cannot perform $2.56 \times 10^8$ measurements on a state that decoheres in 500 $\mu$s. You would need to prepare the same state identically $> 250,000$ times, and this assumes perfect state preparation fidelity (which is typically 99-99.9% per qubit, compounding to $\sim 96\%$ for 4 qubits).

### The Strong Driving Problem

The paper proposes "strongly driven fermion chains far from equilibrium." But strong driving means:

1. **Shorter $T_1$ and $T_2$.** Driving accelerates relaxation. The effective coherence time under strong drive is typically 1-2 orders of magnitude shorter than the idle $T_2$.

2. **Calibration drift.** High-power drives heat the device, shifting qubit frequencies and readout calibration. The 256 measurement settings cannot be assumed to probe the same state.

3. **Readout crosstalk.** Dispersive readout of multiple qubits simultaneously introduces measurement-induced dephasing on unread qubits.

### The Actual Achievable Precision

A realistic estimate for 4-qubit tomography under strong driving:
- Effective $T_2^* \sim 10$ $\mu$s under drive
- State preparation + tomography window $\sim 5$ $\mu$s
- Repetitions per state: $\sim 10^4$ (limited by drift)
- Total per Pauli setting: $\sim 10^4$ shots
- Statistical error on $\langle n_i n_j \rangle$: $\sim 1/\sqrt{10^4} = 0.01$
- Error on $\kappa_{ij}$ after subtraction: $\sim 0.03$ (combined from three terms)
- This is comparable to the signal itself (10-30%) -- i.e., the measurement is noise-limited.

### What Is Needed

To make this experiment credible, the paper must:

1. Specify the exact tomography protocol (which Pauli basis? adaptive or fixed?).
2. Compute the quantum and classical Fisher information for $\kappa_{ij}$ estimation, and derive the Cramer-Rao bound on the number of measurements.
3. Account for state preparation and measurement (SPAM) errors.
4. Show that the total measurement time fits within the effective coherence window under strong driving.
5. Provide an error budget including readout fidelity, crosstalk, and $T_1$ decay during measurement.

### Verdict on System 2

**The claimed precision (10-30% violation detection) is not achievable with current technology without an unrealistically large number of repetitions.** The coherence time bottleneck alone makes this measurement marginal at best.

## Finding 3: BEC Analogue Black Holes -- Non-Diagonal Correlations [FATAL]

### The Paper's Claim

The paper claims (Section V, System 3) that in a BEC analogue black hole, one can measure non-diagonal frequency correlations:

$$C_J(\omega, \omega') \equiv \langle \delta n(\omega) \delta n(\omega')\rangle \neq 0 \quad (\omega \neq \omega')$$

and that this is achievable in "Steinhauer-type BEC experiments."

### What Steinhauer Actually Measured

Steinhauer (Nature Physics 12, 959, 2016) measured the **diagonal** density-density correlation function:

$$\langle \delta n(x) \delta n(x') \rangle$$

integrated over specific spatial regions (inside vs. outside the horizon). The frequency-domain analysis was performed via Fourier transform of the spatial correlator. Critically, Steinhauer measured:

$$S(\omega) = \langle |\delta n(\omega)|^2 \rangle$$

This is the **power spectrum** -- the diagonal element of the frequency-frequency correlation matrix. It contains no phase information between different frequency components.

### What Non-Diagonal Measurement Requires

To measure $C_J(\omega, \omega')$ for $\omega \neq \omega'$, you need:

1. **Phase-sensitive detection.** You need to measure not just the amplitude $|\delta n(\omega)|$ but the relative phase between density fluctuations at different frequencies. This requires a heterodyne or homodyne detection scheme with a local oscillator, not the direct absorption imaging used by Steinhauer.

2. **Simultaneous access to two frequency channels.** You cannot Fourier-transform a single time series and multiply amplitudes, because $\langle \delta n(\omega) \delta n(\omega') \rangle$ for generic non-stationary states is NOT equal to the product of Fourier transforms. You need either:
   - Two independent local probes with cross-correlated readout, or
   - A nonlinear detection scheme (e.g., frequency mixing before detection).

3. **Time resolution better than the inverse frequency difference.** For $\Delta\omega = |\omega - \omega'| \sim 2\pi \times 100$ Hz (typical analogue Hawking frequencies), you need time resolution $\ll 10$ ms AND the coherence time of the BEC must exceed this. The Steinhauer BEC lifetime is $\sim 100$ ms, giving you at best $\sim 10$ independent frequency bins. This is marginal.

4. **A model for the expected $C_J$.** The paper estimates "$C_J/\bar{n} \sim O(1\%)$" but provides no derivation, noting that "a full numerical solution of the Bogoliubov-de Gennes equations for an inhomogeneous flowing condensate" is "pending." Without a predicted frequency dependence, an experimentalist cannot design a detection scheme with the appropriate bandwidth and sensitivity.

### The Deeper Conceptual Problem

The non-diagonal correlation $C_J(\omega, \omega')$ for a stationary Hawking process should be zero by **time-translation invariance**, not by the thermal nature of the spectrum. In a stationary process:

$$\langle \delta n(\omega) \delta n(\omega') \rangle \propto \delta(\omega + \omega')$$

The Hawking radiation from a stationary horizon is stationary. The paper claims "causal disconnection produces $\tilde{C}^I \neq 0$" as the mechanism for $C_J(\omega, \omega') \neq 0$, but causal disconnection in a stationary metric does not break time-translation invariance (the metric is stationary by construction). The $\omega = \omega'$ part of the correlator is the only non-vanishing part for any stationary state, thermal or not.

If $\tilde{C}^I \neq 0$ genuinely breaks stationarity, then:
- This is a dynamical transient effect, not a steady-state property.
- It would occur only during the formation or evaporation of the horizon.
- Its duration is set by the light-crossing time of the horizon, which for BEC analogues is $\sim \mu$m$/c_s \sim 10^{-4}$ s, much shorter than the measurement time.

### Verdict on System 3

**The non-diagonal correlator $C_J(\omega, \omega')$ for $\omega \neq \omega'$ vanishes for a stationary horizon on general grounds of time-translation invariance.** The paper's claim that $\tilde{C}^I \neq 0$ produces this signal is physically unmotivated without a dynamical calculation showing how stationarity is broken. Even if a transient signal exists, its magnitude and duration are not quantified, making experimental design impossible.

## Finding 4: Complete Absence of Error Analysis [FATAL]

### The Problem

For a paper that proposes experimental falsification of standard quantum mechanics, the complete absence of ANY error analysis is disqualifying. No experimentalist would -- or should -- take seriously a prediction that does not come with an error budget.

### What Must Be Provided

For each of the three proposed systems, the paper must address:

**Systematic errors:**
- Calibration uncertainty in the measurement apparatus
- Inhomogeneous broadening in qubit frequencies
- Trap anharmonicity and finite-size effects in BEC
- Edge reconstruction and disorder effects in FQH
- Finite-temperature corrections (all three systems)

**Statistical errors:**
- Shot noise limit for the proposed measurement protocol
- Number of independent repetitions required
- Scaling of SNR with system size $L$

**Decoherence and dephasing:**
- $T_1$ and $T_2$ limits for qubit chains
- BEC atom loss and heating rates
- FQH edge equilibration and quasiparticle poisoning times

**Readout fidelity and back-action:**
- Dispersive readout fidelity for multi-qubit tomography
- Absorption imaging shot noise for BEC density correlations
- Quantum point contact partitioning noise for FQH

### Quantifying a "Discovery"

The paper claims "any statistically significant violation constitutes a discovery." But *what is the threshold for statistical significance*? In particle physics, $5\sigma$ is standard. In condensed matter, $3\sigma$ is often sufficient when combined with systematic error control. What $\sigma$ threshold does this paper propose? Without specifying:
- The expected signal-to-noise ratio
- The number of standard deviations separating the null hypothesis from new physics
- The false positive rate from mundane systematics

... the term "falsifiable" is empty.

## Finding 5: Trivial Explanations for Apparent Violation [MAJOR]

### The Paper's Claim

The paper treats any violation of $\Delta C^R \cdot \Delta C^I \geq \frac{1}{4}|\Delta n|$ as evidence for $\neg(\text{A1}) \vee \neg(\text{A2}) \vee \neg(\text{A3})$ -- i.e., new physics or at least physics beyond the standard framework.

### Mundane Explanations Not Addressed

**(a) Calibration error in density measurement.** $\Delta n = \langle n_n \rangle - \langle n_m \rangle$ is measured by counting atoms (or electrons, or qubit excitations) at two sites. If the calibration of site $m$ and site $n$ differs by even 1%, the measured $\Delta n$ is systematically shifted. Since the bound is proportional to $|\Delta n|$, a calibration error in $\Delta n$ propagates directly to the violation claim. For the FQH system, site-resolved charge sensing with 1% accuracy is science fiction.

**(b) Finite temperature effects.** The paper's null hypothesis verification (85,639 states) assumes either ground states or boundary-driven non-equilibrium steady states (NESS) at exactly specified parameters. BUT:
- Real FQH systems are at $T \sim 20-100$ mK. At $\nu = 1/3$ with a gap $\Delta \sim 2$ K (in GaAs), the thermal quasiparticle population is $\sim e^{-\Delta/T} \sim 10^{-9}$ -- negligible. But in graphene FQH (the system actually cited via Werkmeister), the gap is smaller and thermal effects are larger.
- Superconducting qubits operate at $T \sim 15$ mK, but the *effective* temperature of the qubit under strong driving can be much higher due to drive-induced heating.
- BEC experiments are at $T \sim 10-100$ nK, but the analogue Hawking temperature is $T_H \sim 1$ nK, so thermal contamination of the Hawking signal is severe (signal-to-thermal ratio $\sim 0.1-0.01$).

In all three cases, finite temperature *raises* $\Delta C^R \cdot \Delta C^I$ (more fluctuations), which would make violations *less* likely. This is in the paper's favor, but it also means that any *apparent* violation is more likely to be a systematic underestimate rather than new physics.

**(c) Quasiparticle poisoning.** In FQH systems, bulk quasiparticles can tunnel to the edge, changing the effective edge state population and therefore the measured $\Delta n$. The characteristic poisoning time at $\nu = 1/3$ is $\sim 1-100$ seconds at $T = 20$ mK (dependent on sample quality). But the interferometry measurement takes $\sim$ minutes to hours. Quasiparticle poisoning during the measurement produces a time-dependent $\Delta n$ that can fake a violation.

**(d) Charge noise.** In both FQH and superconducting qubit systems, $1/f$ charge noise produces slow fluctuations in the local potential, shifting site energies and therefore $\Delta n$. This is not "new physics" -- it is the well-known charge noise that all condensed matter experimentalists fight daily. The paper's violation signature would be indistinguishable from charge noise unless the noise spectrum is independently characterized.

**(e) Readout-induced back-action.** In dispersive qubit readout, the measurement tone itself causes ac-Stark shifts and measurement-induced dephasing. The *measured* $\langle n_i \rangle$ is not the pre-measurement expectation value; it is contaminated by the readout process itself. This is a well-known effect (the "measurement-induced state collapse" that the paper itself discusses in a different context).

### Duty of the Authors

A paper claiming to detect "new physics" has the burden of ruling out ALL mundane explanations before invoking exotic ones. The fact that none of (a)-(e) is even mentioned in the manuscript suggests the authors have not consulted experimentalists.

## Finding 6: Numerical "Verification" Is Experimentally Meaningless [MODERATE]

The paper claims "verified for 85,639 quantum states (L = 2-8; 0 violations)." This is a computational exercise performed on the authors' own theoretical model. It does not constitute experimental verification. The word "verified" is misleading -- these are consistency checks, not experimental tests.

More importantly, the $L = 2-8$ range is far too small. Real physical systems have $L \sim 10^3$ (qubit chains), $L \sim 10^5$ (BEC sites), or continuum (FQH edges). The paper provides no scaling analysis of how the bound behaves as $L \to \infty$. For all the reader knows, finite-size effects could produce apparent violations in small systems that disappear in the thermodynamic limit.

The null hypothesis should be supplemented by:
- Scaling of minimum $R \equiv (\Delta C^R \cdot \Delta C^I)/(\frac{1}{4}|\Delta n|)$ with $L$
- Behavior at realistic temperatures ($T > 0$)
- Effect of disorder in the Hamiltonian
- Behavior with open boundary conditions (realistic) vs. the assumed geometry

## The Single Most Important Reason for Rejection

This paper's central contribution is the claim that standard quantum mechanics makes an **experimentally falsifiable** prediction. But none of the three proposed experiments is actually feasible as described:

- **FQH:** The observable ($C^R$, $C^I$) is not what the cited experiments measure. No protocol exists to extract it.
- **Qubits:** The required measurement precision demands more repetitions than coherence time allows.
- **BEC black holes:** The claimed non-diagonal correlation signal likely vanishes for a stationary horizon, and no detection scheme exists anyway.

**A paper whose entire importance rests on experimental testability but which provides no feasible experiment cannot be published in Physical Review D.** The inequality $\Delta C^R \cdot \Delta C^I \geq \frac{1}{4}|\Delta n|$ may be mathematically correct, but calling it "falsifiable" requires actual falsifiability in a real laboratory with real equipment. This threshold has not been met.

---

## What Would Change My Mind

To make the experimental section credible, the authors would need to:

1. **For FQH:** Derive the mapping $\langle c^\dagger_n c_m \rangle \leftrightarrow \langle e^{i\sqrt{\nu}\phi(x)}\rangle$ for edge channels. Propose a specific multi-gate geometry that can independently address edge segments. Calculate the expected signal magnitude for $\nu = 1/3$ in GaAs with realistic device parameters (disorder, finite size, temperature). Provide an error budget that distinguishes anyonic signal from charge noise and quasiparticle poisoning.

2. **For qubits:** Provide a quantum Cramer-Rao analysis for $\kappa_{ij}$ estimation. Demonstrate that the total measurement time (including state preparation, gate operations, and readout) fits within the effective coherence time under strong driving. Account for SPAM errors at the level required for 10% precision on the connected cumulant. Show a complete error budget.

3. **For BEC black holes:** Prove that $\tilde{C}^I \neq 0$ breaks time-translation invariance and estimate the duration and magnitude of the transient signal. Propose a concrete detection scheme (heterodyne, homodyne, or other) with sensitivity estimates. Show that the signal-to-noise ratio including shot noise, thermal contamination, and finite BEC lifetime exceeds 3 for realistic parameters.

4. **For all systems:** Provide a systematic error analysis covering the mundane explanations listed in Finding 5. Specify the statistical significance threshold for a "discovery." Demonstrate that the proposed experiments can reach this threshold.

Until these are provided, the "experimentally testable" claim is a promissory note with no collateral.

---

**Recommendation:** Reject, with encouragement to resubmit after either (a) performing the experiments and reporting results, or (b) collaborating with experimentalists to develop credible, quantitatively detailed measurement proposals for at least one of the three systems.
