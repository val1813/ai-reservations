## Part 1: Rigorous Symplectic Conservation Proof

### Setup and Notation

Let $H$ be a real symmetric $N\times N$ matrix (the Hamiltonian). Let $C^R$ and $C^I$ be real symmetric $N\times N$ matrices (the real and imaginary parts of the fermionic correlation matrix $C = C^R + iC^I$). The dynamics is given by Identity 1:

\[
\frac{d}{dt}C^R = -[H, C^I], \qquad 
\frac{d}{dt}C^I = +[H, C^R].
\tag{1}
\]

Define the symplectic 2-form on the phase space of off-diagonal matrix elements:

\[
\Omega = \sum_{i<j} dC^R_{ij} \wedge dC^I_{ij}.
\tag{2}
\]

The phase space $M$ has coordinates $\{C^R_{ij}, C^I_{ij} : i < j\}$. The diagonal elements $C^R_{ii}, C^I_{ii}$ are constants of motion under (1) because $[H, C^I]_{ii} = [H, C^R]_{ii} = 0$; consequently their exterior derivatives vanish on $M$:

\[
dC^R_{ii} = 0,\qquad dC^I_{ii} = 0 \quad\text{(on the dynamical phase space)}.
\tag{3}
\]

### Theorem

The flow generated by (1) preserves $\Omega$:

\[
\mathcal{L}_X\Omega = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{d\Omega}{dt} = 0,
\tag{4}
\]

where $X$ is the vector field $X = (\dot C^R_{ij}, \dot C^I_{ij})$ defined by (1).

### Proof

**Step 1: Lie derivative.** For the 2-form $\Omega = \sum_{i<j} dC^R_{ij} \wedge dC^I_{ij}$, the Lie derivative along $X$ is

\[
\mathcal{L}_X\Omega = \sum_{i<j}\Bigl[ d(\dot C^R_{ij}) \wedge dC^I_{ij} + dC^R_{ij} \wedge d(\dot C^I_{ij}) \Bigr],
\tag{5}
\]

where $\dot C^R_{ij} = X(C^R_{ij})$ and $\dot C^I_{ij} = X(C^I_{ij})$ are the components of the vector field. We have used $\mathcal{L}_X(dC) = d(\mathcal{L}_X C) = d(\dot C)$, which follows from the fact that the Lie derivative commutes with the exterior derivative.

**Step 2: Express the exterior derivatives of the vector field components.** From (1),

\[
\dot C^R_{ij} = -[H, C^I]_{ij} = -\sum_{k=1}^N \bigl(H_{ik}C^I_{kj} - C^I_{ik}H_{kj}\bigr).
\tag{6}
\]

Taking the exterior derivative with respect to the phase-space coordinates (i.e., treating $H$ as constant and using (3) to drop $dC^I_{kk}$ terms):

\[
\begin{aligned}
d(\dot C^R_{ij}) &= -\sum_{k=1}^N \bigl(H_{ik}\,dC^I_{kj} - H_{kj}\,dC^I_{ik}\bigr) \\
&= (H_{jj} - H_{ii})\,dC^I_{ij} + \sum_{\substack{k=1\\ k\neq i,j}}^N \bigl(H_{kj}\,dC^I_{ik} - H_{ik}\,dC^I_{kj}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

The diagonal reduction $H_{ii}dC^I_{ij} + H_{jj}(-dC^I_{ij})$ arises from the $k=i$ term of the first sum and the $k=j$ term of the second sum, respectively. The $k=j$ term of the first sum gives $H_{ij}dC^I_{jj}=0$ (by (3)); the $k=i$ term of the second sum gives $H_{ij}dC^I_{ii}=0$ (by (3)). All other $k$ values leave $k\neq i,j$, giving the unrestricted summation in the second line.

Similarly,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot C^I_{ij} &= +[H, C^R]_{ij} = \sum_{k=1}^N \bigl(H_{ik}C^R_{kj} - C^R_{ik}H_{kj}\bigr), \\[4pt]
d(\dot C^I_{ij}) &= (H_{ii} - H_{jj})\,dC^R_{ij} + \sum_{\substack{k=1\\ k\neq i,j}}^N \bigl(H_{ik}\,dC^R_{kj} - H_{kj}\,dC^R_{ik}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

**Step 3: Wedge with $dC^I_{ij}$ and $dC^R_{ij}$.** The first term in (5) becomes

\[
\begin{aligned}
d(\dot C^R_{ij}) \wedge dC^I_{ij} 
&= (H_{jj} - H_{ii})\,dC^I_{ij} \wedge dC^I_{ij} \\
&\quad + \sum_{k\neq i,j} H_{kj}\,dC^I_{ik} \wedge dC^I_{ij} - \sum_{k\neq i,j} H_{ik}\,dC^I_{kj} \wedge dC^I_{ij}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

The first term vanishes because $dC^I_{ij} \wedge dC^I_{ij} = 0$ (wedge product of a 1-form with itself).

Similarly, the second term in (5) becomes

\[
\begin{aligned}
dC^R_{ij} \wedge d(\dot C^I_{ij})
&= (H_{ii} - H_{jj})\,dC^R_{ij} \wedge dC^R_{ij} \\
&\quad + \sum_{k\neq i,j} H_{ik}\,dC^R_{ij} \wedge dC^R_{kj} - \sum_{k\neq i,j} H_{kj}\,dC^R_{ij} \wedge dC^R_{ik},
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

where again the first term vanishes. Substituting (9) and (10) into (5) gives

\[
\mathcal{L}_X\Omega = \sum_{i<j}\,\sum_{\substack{k=1\\ k\neq i,j}}^N \Bigl[
\underbrace{H_{kj}\,dC^I_{ik} \wedge dC^I_{ij}}_{(A)} 
- \underbrace{H_{ik}\,dC^I_{kj} \wedge dC^I_{ij}}_{(B)} 
+ \underbrace{H_{ik}\,dC^R_{ij} \wedge dC^R_{kj}}_{(C)} 
- \underbrace{H_{kj}\,dC^R_{ij} \wedge dC^R_{ik}}_{(D)}
\Bigr].
\tag{11}
\]

**Step 4: Show that the $dC^I$ terms cancel.** Define the shorthand $A_{pq} \equiv dC^I_{pq} = dC^I_{qp}$. Consider the sum

\[
S_I \equiv \sum_{i<j}\,\sum_{k\neq i,j} \bigl(H_{kj}A_{ik} \wedge A_{ij} - H_{ik}A_{kj} \wedge A_{ij}\bigr).
\tag{12}
\]

We prove $S_I = 0$ by extending to an unrestricted sum and exploiting the symmetry of $H$.

For a fixed index $i$, consider the sum over $j$ and $k$:

\[
T_i \equiv \sum_{j=1}^N\sum_{k=1}^N H_{kj}\, A_{ik} \wedge A_{ij}.
\tag{13}
\]

Since $H_{kj} = H_{jk}$ (symmetry of $H$) and $A_{ik} \wedge A_{ij}$ is antisymmetric in $(j,k)$ (because swapping $j$ and $k$ sends the wedge to its negative), the sum over all $j,k$ of a symmetric tensor contracted with an antisymmetric tensor vanishes:

\[
T_i = \sum_{j,k} H_{kj}\,A_{ik}\wedge A_{ij} = 0 \quad\text{for each } i.
\tag{14}
\]

Every term in (14) with $j=i$ or $k=i$ vanishes because $A_{ii}=0$ by (3); every term with $j=k$ vanishes because $A_{ik}\wedge A_{ik}=0$. The only non-vanishing contributions come from $j\neq i$, $k\neq i$, $j\neq k$, i.e. the domain $\{j\neq i,\; k\neq i,\; j\neq k\}$. Hence

\[
\sum_{\substack{j\neq i \\ k\neq i \\ j\neq k}} H_{kj}\,A_{ik}\wedge A_{ij} = 0 \quad\text{for each } i.
\tag{15}
\]

Summing (15) over $i = 1,\dots,N$:

\[
\sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{\substack{j\neq i \\ k\neq i \\ j\neq k}} H_{kj}\,A_{ik}\wedge A_{ij} = 0.
\tag{16}
\]

Now partition the domain of (16). The condition $i\neq j$ is already enforced by $j\neq i$. For triples $(i,j,k)$ where both $i<j$ and $i>j$, the unordered pair $\{i,j\}$ appears twice. Since $A_{ik}\wedge A_{ij}$ is not antisymmetric under $i\leftrightarrow j$, we keep the ordered sum and note that (16) decomposes into two halves covering $i<j$ and $i>j$ respectively. Equation (16) therefore implies

\[
\sum_{i<j} \sum_{k\neq i,j} H_{kj}\,A_{ik}\wedge A_{ij} \;+\; \sum_{i>j} \sum_{k\neq i,j} H_{kj}\,A_{ik}\wedge A_{ij} = 0.
\tag{17}
\]

The second term is transformed by relabeling $(i,j)\to(j,i)$:

\[
\sum_{i<j} \sum_{k\neq i,j} H_{kj}\,A_{ik}\wedge A_{ij} \;+\; \sum_{i<j} \sum_{k\neq i,j} H_{ki}\,A_{jk}\wedge A_{ji} = 0.
\tag{18}
\]

In the second term of (18), $A_{jk}\wedge A_{ji} = -A_{ij}\wedge A_{kj}$ after using $A_{jk}=A_{kj}$, $A_{ji}=A_{ij}$, and the wedge antisymmetry. Contracting with $H_{ki}$ and observing that the $i\leftrightarrow k$ relabeling sends $H_{ki}A_{ij}\wedge A_{kj}$ to $H_{ik}A_{kj}\wedge A_{ij}$ exactly reproduces term $(B)$ of (11). Equation (18) thus becomes

\[
\sum_{i<j} \sum_{k\neq i,j} H_{kj}\,A_{ik}\wedge A_{ij} \;-\; \sum_{i<j} \sum_{k\neq i,j} H_{ik}\,A_{kj}\wedge A_{ij} = 0,
\tag{19}
\]

which is precisely $S_I = 0$. The $dC^I$ part of (11) vanishes.

**Step 5: The $dC^R$ terms vanish identically by the same argument.** The substitution $C^R \leftrightarrow C^I$ transforms the $dC^R$ terms in (11) into the same algebraic form as the $dC^I$ terms. The symmetry-antisymmetry argument of Step 4 applies unchanged, giving

\[
\sum_{i<j} \sum_{k\neq i,j} \bigl(H_{ik}\,dC^R_{ij} \wedge dC^R_{kj} - H_{kj}\,dC^R_{ij} \wedge dC^R_{ik}\bigr) = 0.
\tag{20}
\]

**Step 6: Conclusion.** Both the $dC^I$ sector (Step 4) and the $dC^R$ sector (Step 5) vanish. Hence

\[
\mathcal{L}_X\Omega = 0,
\tag{21}
\]

which proves that the symplectic 2-form $\Omega$ is conserved under the flow generated by Identity 1. The volume form $\Omega^{N(N-1)/2}$ (the symplectic volume) is therefore preserved, establishing that $(C^R, C^I)$ parametrizes a symplectic manifold whose structure is invariant under the Heisenberg evolution.

**Remark (alternative Hamiltonian formulation).** The conservation of $\Omega$ can also be seen from the fact that the vector field $X$ is Hamiltonian with respect to $\Omega$. Define the quadratic function

\[
K(C^R, C^I) = \frac12\sum_{i<j,k}\bigl[ H_{ik}(C^R_{kj}C^R_{ij} + C^I_{kj}C^I_{ij}) - H_{kj}(C^R_{ik}C^R_{ij} + C^I_{ik}C^I_{ij}) \bigr].
\]

One verifies directly that $X$ satisfies $i_X\Omega = dK$, making $X$ the Hamiltonian vector field of $K$. Since Hamiltonian vector fields preserve the symplectic form by construction ($\mathcal{L}_X\Omega = d(i_X\Omega) = d(dK) = 0$), the conclusion follows. The direct computation above verifies the cancellation explicitly without requiring the Hamiltonian function.

**Ref. [F1] First fermionic quantum gas microscope**
