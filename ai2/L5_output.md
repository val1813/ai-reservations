## LP-Candidate-L5-1: [Type A] — The Hubble Tension: ΛCDM Cannot Explain the Local vs CMB H0 Discrepancy

**Anomaly:** The local (late-universe) expansion rate H0 = 73.50 +/- 0.81 km/s/Mpc is systematically higher than the early-universe CMB-extrapolated value H0 = 67.27 +/- 0.60 km/s/Mpc. The discrepancy persists across ALL measurement techniques and cannot be attributed to any single systematic error.

**Standard Theory Prediction:** ΛCDM cosmology predicts that the same H0 should be inferred from CMB power spectra (assuming ΛCDM evolution) as from direct distance-ladder measurements in the local universe. The two values should agree to within statistical error.

**Significance:** 5-7σ, depending on dataset combination. The 2026 H0 Distance Network (H0DN) Collaboration measured H0 = 73.50 +/- 0.81 km/s/Mpc (~1% precision). Combined with Planck 2018 (67.27 +/- 0.60), the discrepancy reaches 5.5σ. Individual analyses quote 5-7σ. This means <1 in 3.5 million chance of statistical fluke.

**Independent Confirmation Groups (≥2, ACTUALLY DOZENS):**
- **H0DN Collaboration (2026):** 11+ independent distance indicators (Cepheids, TRGB, Mira, megamasers, SN Ia, SN II, SBF, Tully-Fisher, Fundamental Plane) in unified network framework. H0 = 73.50 +/- 0.81.
- **SH0ES / Riess et al. (2009-2025):** Cepheid+SN Ia ladder, consistently H0 ~73-74 km/s/Mpc, JWST confirmation in 2024-25.
- **TRGB / Freedman et al. (2019-2025):** Tip of Red Giant Branch method, H0 ~69-70 km/s/Mpc (intermediate but still > CMB).
- **H0LiCOW / TDCOSMO (2020-2024):** Strong gravitational lensing time delays, H0 ~73.3 +/- 1.8.
- **Megamaser Cosmology Project (MCP):** H2O megamasers in AGN disks, H0 ~73.9 +/- 3.0.
- **Planck Collaboration (2018):** CMB TT+TE+EE+lowE+lensing, H0 = 67.27 +/- 0.60 (ΛCDM-extrapolated).
- **ACT Collaboration (2020-2024):** Atacama Cosmology Telescope, consistent with Planck.
- **DESI (2024-2025):** BAO + CMB, consistent with Planck at low H0.

JWST cross-checks of Cepheids in 2024-25 matched HST results within 1-2%, eliminating several proposed systematic error sources.

**Core Contradiction:** The universe's expansion rate measured TODAY (73.5 km/s/Mpc) does not match what ΛCDM predicts it SHOULD be, given the conditions measured in the earliest observable universe (67.3 km/s/Mpc). Either (a) ΛCDM is incomplete/wrong, or (b) every independent local measurement technique shares an unknown, cross-cutting systematic error of ~9% — which the H0DN network analysis effectively rules out.

**Sub-propositions (≥3):**
1. **P1 — Early Universe Extrapolation:** Does the Planck/ACT ΛCDM inference of H0 depend on assumptions about the pre-recombination universe (e.g., dark energy equation of state, number of relativistic species Neff, primordial helium abundance Yp)? This is attackable by testing extensions to ΛCDM against CMB data.
2. **P2 — Late Universe Systematics:** Is there a residual systematic error in the local distance ladder that affects ALL 11+ independent distance indicators? This is attackable by cross-correlating different distance indicators and searching for correlated residuals — already partially done by H0DN.
3. **P3 — New Physics in the Late Universe:** Does the tension require a modification to the late-time expansion history (e.g., phantom dark energy, interacting dark matter-dark energy, decaying dark matter, modified gravity)? This is attackable with existing DESI BAO + Pantheon+ SN Ia data, which can constrain late-time deviations from ΛCDM.
4. **P4 — New Physics in the Early Universe:** Can early-universe modifications (early dark energy, extra relativistic species, primordial magnetic fields, non-standard recombination) resolve the tension without breaking CMB consistency? This is attackable by fitting EDE/ΔNeff models to Planck + ACT + SPT data.
5. **P5 — Independent Arbiter (Gravitational Waves):** Will standard siren measurements from LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA neutron star mergers eventually favor one H0 value over the other? Current data is insufficient but the method is fundamentally independent of both CMB and distance ladder.

**Journal Level:** **Nature Physics / Physical Review Letters** — 5σ+ statistical significance, affects all of cosmology + particle physics, 13+ years of persistent confrontation across dozens of independent measurement teams, multiple JWST cross-validations ruling out systematics. Called a "full-blown crisis" by leading cosmologists. If confirmed as new physics, it would be a Nobel-worthy revision to the standard model of cosmology.

## LP-Candidate-L5-2: [Type A] — Strange Metal T-Linear Resistivity: 40-Year Universality Puzzle Across 6+ Material Families

**Anomaly:** The electrical resistivity ρ(T) ∝ T (exactly linear in temperature, not T² as in Fermi liquid theory) is observed over up to 5 decades of temperature in at least six distinct strongly correlated material families: hole-doped cuprates, electron-doped cuprates, heavy fermion compounds, ruthenates, iron pnictides, organic charge-transfer salts, and now even ferromagnetic metals (Zhan PRL 2025). The scattering rate universally reaches the "Planckian limit" ℏ/τ ~ k_B T, suggesting a fundamental bound on dissipation that transcends microscopic details.

**Standard Theory Prediction:** Landau Fermi liquid theory (the standard model of metals) predicts ρ(T) ∝ T² from electron-electron scattering at low temperatures, with a material-specific prefactor. At high temperatures (T > Θ_D), electron-phonon scattering gives ρ(T) ∝ T. Strange metals exhibit T-linear resistivity over temperature ranges far too broad (sometimes 1 K to 1000 K) and with slopes far too universal to be conventional electron-phonon scattering.

**Significance:** Decades of data, hundreds of independent measurements. Not a statistical fluctuation — a systematic, reproducible violation of Fermi liquid theory across an entire class of materials. The Planckian scattering rate k_B T/ℏ appears as a universal upper bound with no known exceptions in the strange metal regime.

**Independent Confirmation Groups (≥2, ACTUALLY DOZENS):**
- **Cuprates (1987-present):** T-linear resistivity in LSCO, YBCO, BSCCO, Hg-cuprates; confirmed by Hussey, Ando, Boebinger, Mackenzie, Greene, and many others over 35+ years. Shekhter et al. (2025) showed doping-independent Planckian relaxation (1/τ ∝ k_B T/ℏ, independent of doping p).
- **Heavy Fermions (2000-present):** CeCoIn5, YbRh2Si2, CeCu6-xAux near quantum critical points; T-linear resistivity spanning 2+ decades of T. Confirmed by von Lohneysen, Gegenwart, Si, Coleman groups.
- **Ruthenates (2004-present):** Sr2RuO4 above ~800 K, Sr3Ru2O7 near its metamagnetic quantum critical point. Confirmed by Mackenzie/Hussey groups.
- **Iron Pnictides (2009-present):** BaFe2(As1-xPx)2, Ba(Fe1-xCox)2As2 near optimal doping. Confirmed by Kasahara, Shibauchi, Matsuda groups.
- **Organics (2000-present):** κ-(BEDT-TTF)2X near the Mott transition. Confirmed by Toyota, Kanoda groups.
- **Kagome Metals (2026):** Ni3In — zero-bias peak-dip structure tracking strange metal state. Weizmann Institute (Nature Physics 2026).
- **Ferromagnetic Metals (2025):** Zhan et al. PRL — first observation of strange metal T-linear resistivity in a ferromagnet, extending universality to an entirely new magnetic class.

**Core Contradiction:** Fermi liquid theory — the foundational theory of metallic behavior — predicts ρ ∝ T², yet strange metals universally exhibit ρ ∝ T over temperature ranges spanning 3-5 orders of magnitude. The universality of the Planckian scattering rate (ℏ/τ ~ k_B T) across materials with radically different chemistry, dimensionality, and band structure demands a theory that transcends microscopic details — but after 40 years, no such theory has achieved consensus.

**Sub-propositions (≥3):**
1. **P1 — Is T-linear resistivity always associated with a quantum critical point (QCP)?** Some strange metals are near a QCP (heavy fermions, pnictides), but the overdoped cuprates and Sr2RuO4 show extended T-linear behavior without an identifiable QCP. Attackable by systematic quantum oscillation/transport studies mapping the phase diagrams of candidate materials.
2. **P2 — Is Planckian dissipation a fundamental bound or an emergent coincidence?** The saturation of ℏ/τ at k_B T/ℏ suggests a universal bound on how fast a quantum system can relax — akin to the viscosity bound in holography. Attackable by comparing scattering rates across ALL known strange metals, including the new ferromagnetic case (Zhan 2025).
3. **P3 — Can holographic (AdS/CFT) models produce quantitative predictions that distinguish them from quantum critical + disorder models?** Holographic models predict specific signatures (e.g., anomalous cyclotron frequency in magnetotransport) that may be testable in clean strange metal samples. Attackable with existing high-field magnetotransport data.
4. **P4 — Does the charge response (plasmon dispersion, EELS) discriminate between theories?** Recent controversy: T-EELS on Bi-2212 (arXiv:2602.02348) finds overdamped plasmons, while R-EELS on Sr2RuO4 (Nature Comms 2025) shows NO overdamping — directly contradicting holographic predictions. Attackable by systematic EELS on additional strange metal systems.

**Journal Level:** **Nature Physics** — 40-year central puzzle in condensed matter physics, affects understanding of strongly correlated electrons, high-Tc superconductivity, quantum criticality, and potentially links to holographic quantum gravity. Six or more independent material families confirm the universality. The controversy between conflicting EELS results on different strange metals provides an active, attackable confrontation.

## LP-Candidate-L5-3: [Type A] — Quantum Oscillations in Insulators: Neutral Fermions Violate the Metal-Insulator Dichotomy

**Anomaly:** Quantum oscillations — the periodic variation of resistivity, magnetization, and heat capacity with inverse magnetic field — are observed in electrical insulators YbB12 and SmB6. Quantum oscillations are the "gold standard" signature of a Fermi surface, which should only exist in metals. These materials have robust charge gaps (~10-20 meV) yet exhibit Landau level quantization identical in form to that of conventional metals. The 2024 thermodynamic data on YbB12 shows fermionic excitation signatures that violate the Wiedemann-Franz law by a factor of ~1000.

**Standard Theory Prediction:** Quantum oscillations require a Fermi surface — a sharp discontinuity in the electron momentum distribution at the Fermi energy. Insulators, by definition, have a filled valence band and empty conduction band, hence no Fermi surface. An insulator should show NO quantum oscillations. The standard band theory of solids (DFT/GW) predicts a gap in both SmB6 and YbB12, and experiments confirm the charge gap — yet the oscillations persist.

**Significance:** Multiple σ from decades of data; the 2024 Sato et al. thermodynamic signatures (double-peak specific heat, magnetocaloric effect scaling as 4.8 k_B T) are high-precision and leave little room for alternative explanations. The Wiedemann-Franz law violation (thermal conductivity ~1000x too large for the electrical resistivity) is a direct contradiction of standard quasiparticle theory.

**Independent Confirmation Groups:**
- **SmB6 (2012-2020):** Quantum oscillations in magnetic torque (Sebastian group, Cambridge; Tan et al.), later confirmed by multiple groups (Li, Fisk, etc.). Proposed explanations: topological surface states (excluded by bulk-sensitive probes), neutral fermions from Kondo breakdown.
- **YbB12 (2018-2024):** Quantum oscillations in resistivity AND magnetic torque (Sato, Xiang, et al.); metallic thermal conductivity with insulating electrical resistivity (Wiedemann-Franz violation by ~1000x, Sato 2019); thermodynamic double-peak signatures confirming fermionic quasiparticles (Sato et al., Nature Comms 2024, corrected 2025). 3D bulk origin confirmed by fourfold rotational symmetry of the signal.

**Core Contradiction:** Standard band theory says "insulators have no Fermi surface, therefore no quantum oscillations." But two completely different materials (SmB6, YbB12) show robust, bulk quantum oscillations with thermodynamic signatures identical to fermionic Landau quantization. The leading explanation — electron fractionalization into charge-neutral fermions (spinons) — would be a discovery on par with the fractional quantum Hall effect, but the microscopic mechanism remains unknown 13+ years after the first observation.

**Sub-propositions (≥3):**
1. **P1 — Do the quantum oscillations in SmB6 and YbB12 originate from topological surface states or the bulk?** YbB12 data now strongly favors bulk origin (fourfold symmetry, thermal signatures). Attackable by comparing surface-sensitive vs bulk-sensitive probes in the same samples.
2. **P2 — Is the neutral fermion a spinon from Kondo breakdown, an excitonic insulator quasiparticle, or a composite fermion from a topological phase transition?** Multiple competing theories exist with different predictions for the field-dependence of the Landau level gap. Attackable by comparing dϵ/dB values extracted from specific heat (Sato 2024: 0.117-0.258 meV/T) against theoretical predictions.
3. **P3 — Is the phenomenon universal to mixed-valence insulators, or specific to SmB6/YbB12?** If electron fractionalization is a general phenomenon, it should appear in other Kondo insulators (e.g., Ce3Bi4Pt3, FeSi). Attackable by searching for quantum oscillations in candidate materials.
4. **P4 — Does the thermal Hall effect provide a smoking gun?** Sato et al. found negligible thermal Hall conductivity up to 12 T despite metallic longitudinal thermal transport — this is theoretically unexpected. Attackable by extending thermal Hall measurements to higher fields/angles.

**Journal Level:** **Nature Physics** — 13+ years of persistent anomaly across two independent material systems, direct violation of the metal-insulator dichotomy and Wiedemann-Franz law, unambiguous thermodynamic signatures (2024 data), profound implications for understanding electron fractionalization in correlated materials.

## LP-Candidate-L5-4: [Type B] — Majorana Zero Modes: 25-Year Theoretical Prediction With No Unambiguous Experimental Detection

**Anomaly (Type B — Theory → Experiment):** Majorana zero modes (MZMs) — exotic quasiparticles that are their own antiparticles, obeying non-Abelian braiding statistics with potential applications in topological quantum computing — were predicted by Kitaev (2001) to emerge in 1D topological superconductors. Despite 25 years of intense theoretical work (thousands of papers) and numerous experimental claims, no experiment has unambiguously demonstrated MZMs. The field has been marked by high-profile retractions (Nature 2018 paper retracted 2021, second paper retracted 2022) and an active 2025-2026 controversy over Microsoft's "Majorana 1" topological qubit claims, where Nature's own editorial board noted the results "do not represent evidence for the presence of Majorana zero modes."

**Standard Theory Prediction:** MZMs should appear as zero-bias conductance peaks in tunneling spectroscopy of semiconductor-superconductor nanowires (or Fe chains on Pb), with a quantized zero-bias peak height of 2e²/h. They should exhibit the fractional Josephson effect (4π-periodic current-phase relation, manifested as missing odd Shapiro steps), and non-Abelian braiding statistics in multi-wire networks.

**Significance (Null Result):** Not a statistical fluctuation — a systematic failure of proposed detection signatures to cleanly distinguish MZMs from trivial Andreev bound states. A 2025 SciPost paper demonstrated that missing odd-order Shapiro steps (considered a key MZM signature) also appear in topologically trivial junctions. Analysis suggests up to 73% of reported "Majorana signatures" may be trivial Andreev bound states.

**Independent Theory Papers Predicting MZMs (≥3):**
- Kitaev (2001), Phys. Usp. — Original proposal for MZMs in 1D p-wave superconductors (12,000+ citations).
- Lutchyn, Sau, Das Sarma (2010), PRL — Semiconductor-superconductor nanowire proposal (3,000+ citations).
- Oreg, Refael, von Oppen (2010), PRL — Independent nanowire proposal (2,500+ citations).
- Alicea (2012), Rep. Prog. Phys. — Comprehensive review of MZM platforms.
- Beenakker (2013), Annu. Rev. Condens. Matter Phys. — Search for Majorana fermions review.
- Nayak, Simon, Stern, Freedman, Das Sarma (2008), Rev. Mod. Phys. — Non-Abelian anyons and topological quantum computation (4,500+ citations).

**Core Contradiction:** An elegant theoretical prediction, with profound implications for both fundamental physics and quantum computing, has motivated ~25 years of experiments. Yet a Nature 2018 paper claiming MZM detection was retracted in 2021; a follow-up was retracted in 2022; and the 2025 Microsoft "Majorana 1" claim was published with Nature's editorial caveat that the results "do not represent evidence for the presence of Majorana zero modes." The central problem — distinguishing MZMs from trivial Andreev bound states — remains unresolved.

**Sub-propositions (≥3):**
1. **P1 — Does ANY proposed experimental signature CLEANLY distinguish MZMs from trivial Andreev bound states?** Zero-bias peaks, conductance quantization, missing Shapiro steps, and Coulomb blockade spectroscopy have each been shown (in 2025 papers) to admit trivial explanations. Attackable by systematic theoretical analysis of false-positive rates for each proposed signature.
2. **P2 — Is the "topological gap protocol" (used by Microsoft) a reliable diagnostic?** The 2025 SciPost paper showed finite-size biases in the topological gap protocol can produce false positives. Attackable by testing the protocol on well-characterized trivial systems.
3. **P3 — Can any existing published dataset be REANALYZED to confirm or refute MZM claims with higher confidence?** The 2018/2021 retractions demonstrate that reanalysis can overturn claimed MZM detections. Attackable by systematic reanalysis of published MZM datasets with transparent statistical methods.
4. **P4 — Is there a fundamental no-go theorem (analogous to Bell's theorem) that would establish a definitive MZM test?** If trivial Andreev bound states can ALWAYS mimic MZM signatures for any single measurement, then topological protection is fundamentally untestable in a single wire. This is a theoretical question attackable by constructing adversarial examples.

**Journal Level:** **Nature Physics / Physical Review Letters** — 25-year confrontation between an enormously influential theoretical prediction (>20,000 citations across key theory papers) and experimental null results punctuated by retractions. The 2025 Microsoft controversy brought this to public attention. The question of whether MZMs have EVER been detected is one of the most consequential open problems in condensed matter physics, with direct implications for the feasibility of topological quantum computing.

## LP-Candidate-L5-5: [Type B] — Supersymmetry at the TeV Scale: 16 Years of LHC Null Results vs Naturalness Predictions

**Anomaly (Type B — Theory → Experiment):** Supersymmetry (SUSY) — the most studied extension of the Standard Model of particle physics — was predicted by the "naturalness" argument to produce superpartner particles at the TeV scale, observable at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC). After 16 years of LHC operations (Run 1: 2010-2012; Run 2: 2015-2018; Run 3: 2022-2025), no superpartner has been observed. Gluino mass limits have been pushed to ~2.4-2.6 TeV, squark limits to ~1.7-2.4 TeV, and electroweakino limits to ~1 TeV — far above the ~TeV scale where "natural" SUSY was expected.

**Standard Theory Prediction:** SUSY with R-parity conservation predicts pair-produced superpartners (squarks, gluinos, sleptons, electroweakinos) that decay via cascades to the lightest supersymmetric particle (LSP) — a dark matter candidate. The Barbieri-Giudice naturalness criterion requires superpartner masses near the electroweak scale (~100 GeV - 1 TeV) to cancel the Higgs boson's quadratic divergence without fine-tuning. Prior to the LHC, many theorists expected gluinos at 300-500 GeV and stops below 1 TeV.

**Significance (Null Result):** Multiple independent ATLAS and CMS searches across dozens of channels (jets + MET, leptons + MET, photons + MET, same-sign leptons, displaced vertices, long-lived particles). Total analyzed luminosity exceeds 140 fb⁻¹ per experiment from Run 2 alone, with Run 3 adding more. The null results are systematic, reproducible, and increasingly constraining.

**Independent Theory Papers Predicting TeV-Scale SUSY (≥3):**
- Dimopoulos & Georgi (1981), Nucl. Phys. B — Softly broken supersymmetry and SU(5) unification.
- Barbieri & Giudice (1988), Nucl. Phys. B — Quantitative naturalness criterion (upper bounds on superpartner masses).
- Ellis, Enqvist, Nanopoulos, Zwirner (1986) — Phenomenology of the MSSM at colliders.
- Baer, Barger, Serce, Tata — Natural SUSY benchmark scenarios (2010s).
- Hall, Pinner, Ruderman (2012) — "Natural SUSY" with light stops, higgsinos, and gluinos below ~1.5 TeV.
- CMS & ATLAS collaborations — ~100+ published search papers (2010-2025) finding no significant deviations from SM.

**Core Contradiction:** The "naturalness" argument — which made SUSY the dominant BSM paradigm for 40 years — predicts superpartners near the electroweak scale to protect the Higgs mass from radiative corrections. After 16 years of LHC operations, the simplest natural SUSY scenarios (gluinos < 1.5 TeV, stops < 700 GeV) are excluded at high confidence. The theory has retreated: surviving models invoke decoupled first/second generation scalars at 20-40 TeV, compressed spectra with small mass splittings, or R-parity violation — scenarios that evade existing searches but also abandon the original naturalness motivation. As Constantin, Kraml & Mahmoudi (Nucl. Phys. B, 2025) ask: "Has the LHC ruled out supersymmetry — really?"

**Sub-propositions (≥3):**
1. **P1 — At what gluino/stop mass does naturalness become "falsified" by any reasonable definition?** Quantify the Barbieri-Giudice fine-tuning measure Δ as a function of excluded mass range. Attackable by computing Δ for the surviving parameter space given current LHC limits.
2. **P2 — Do compressed SUSY scenarios (small mass splittings, soft decay products) escape detection OR are they ruled out by monojet/soft-lepton searches?** The CMS compressed SUSY program (2025) concluded no excesses found. Attackable by combining all compressed search results to compute global exclusion in the compressed parameter space.
3. **P3 — Is the "decoupled scalars" scenario (Bae et al., PRD 2025) theoretically consistent?** Arguments that charge/color breaking minima exclude certain regions of parameter space. Attackable by full numerical scan of the scalar potential for decoupled-spectrum MSSM.
4. **P4 — If the HL-LHC (3000 fb⁻¹) finds nothing, at what luminosity does SUSY cross the threshold of "effectively falsified" for all natural parameter space?** Extrapolate current limits to 3000 fb⁻¹ with realistic systematics, and compute the remaining Δ < 30 parameter space.

**Journal Level:** **Nature Physics / Physical Review Letters** — 40 years of theoretical dominance (SUSY was the consensus BSM paradigm), 16 years of systematic null results from the world's largest experiment, foundational implications for the naturalness principle in quantum field theory, and direct consequences for the future of high-energy physics collider programs.

## LP-Candidate-L5-6: [Type A] — LSND/MiniBooNE Anomalies After Sterile Neutrino Exclusion: 30-Year Anomaly With Dying Solution

**Anomaly:** The LSND experiment (1993-1998) observed a 3.8σ excess of electron antineutrinos in a muon antineutrino beam. The MiniBooNE experiment (2002-present) confirmed the excess at 4.8σ. For 30 years, the leading explanation was an eV-scale sterile neutrino oscillating with the active flavors — but the MicroBooNE (Nature 2025) and KATRIN (Nature 2025) experiments have now excluded the sterile neutrino solution at high confidence.

**Standard Theory Prediction:** The three-neutrino Standard Model predicts NO excess of electron neutrinos in a muon neutrino beam at short baselines. Sterile neutrinos would produce such an excess through νμ → νe oscillations with Δm² ~ 1 eV².

**Significance:** LSND 3.8σ; MiniBooNE 4.8σ; combined significance is very large. The 2025 MicroBooNE result rules out the sterile neutrino interpretation at 2-3σ (compared to MiniBooNE's preferred region).

**Independent Confirmation Groups:**
- **LSND (1993-1998):** 3.8σ electron antineutrino excess in muon decay-at-rest beam.
- **MiniBooNE (2002-present):** 4.8σ electron-neutrino-like excess, confirming LSND.
- **MicroBooNE (2025):** Two-beam analysis (BNB + NuMI) finds NO excess consistent with sterile neutrino oscillations — excluding the sterile neutrino interpretation of MiniBooNE.
- **KATRIN (2025):** 36 million tritium beta-decay electrons show no sterile neutrino kink.
- **Gallium Anomaly (1990s, updated 2022):** ~20% deficit in electron neutrino detection from radioactive sources in gallium — independent anomaly, also unexplained.

**Core Contradiction:** The LSND/MiniBooNE excess is observationally robust (combined >5σ), but the theoretically favored explanation (eV-scale sterile neutrino) has been excluded by MicroBooNE and KATRIN. The anomalies remain — they just have no explanation. The Quanta Magazine headline (April 2026) captured the situation: "Experiments Ring the 'Death Knell' for Sterile Neutrinos." But as Janet Conrad (MIT) asks: "What else is it?"

**Sub-propositions (≥3):**
1. **P1 — Are the LSND and MiniBooNE excesses produced by the SAME physical mechanism, given their different beam energies and neutrino modes (antineutrino vs neutrino)?** Attackable by joint fits to LSND and MiniBooNE data, checking consistency of the excess as a function of neutrino energy.
2. **P2 — Can photon misidentification in MiniBooNE's Cherenkov detector account for the excess?** The MiniBooNE detector cannot distinguish electrons from single photons on an event-by-event basis. MicroBooNE's superior e/γ separation found fewer events than MiniBooNE predicted. Attackable by detailed GEANT4 simulation of photon backgrounds.
3. **P3 — Do heavier sterile neutrinos (> eV scale) evade KATRIN/MicroBooNE constraints while still explaining LSND/MiniBooNE?** KATRIN's TRISTAN upgrade (2026) will search for keV-scale sterile neutrinos. Attackable by global fits to LSND + MiniBooNE + MicroBooNE data in 3+N models with N > 1.
4. **P4 — Is the gallium anomaly caused by incorrect nuclear matrix elements for the ν_e + ⁷¹Ga → ⁷¹Ge + e⁻ cross section?** This could be tested by an independent cross-section measurement using a ⁵¹Cr source and direct measurement of ⁷¹Ge production.

**Journal Level:** **Physical Review Letters / Nature Physics** — 30-year anomaly, two independent experiments confirming the excess (4.8σ), two newer experiments excluding the leading explanation. This is a genuine Type A: experiments show an effect that theory cannot currently explain. However, the "systematics unknown" risk is high given MiniBooNE's poor e/γ discrimination. The 2025 death of the sterile neutrino solution elevates rather than diminishes the puzzle. **Risk:** This may be resolved by improved photon background modeling rather than new physics.

## LP-Candidate-L5-7: [Type A] — S8/σ8 Tension: Structure Growth in the Late Universe Is Too Weak

**Anomaly:** Galaxy surveys and weak gravitational lensing measurements consistently find the amplitude of matter density fluctuations (parameterized as S8 = σ8(Ωm/0.3)^0.5) to be systematically lower (S8 ~ 0.74-0.76) than predicted from the CMB under ΛCDM (S8 ~ 0.83). The late universe appears ~8% less "clumpy" than the early universe CMB extrapolation predicts.

**Standard Theory Prediction:** ΛCDM extrapolation from Planck CMB (z~1100) predicts S8 = 0.832 +/- 0.013. Late-universe structure growth measurements should agree with this value.

**Significance:** 2-3σ, depending on dataset combination. Weaker than the Hubble tension, but persistent across multiple surveys.

**Independent Confirmation Groups:**
- **KiDS (Kilo-Degree Survey):** S8 ~ 0.76 +/- 0.02 (2020-2024).
- **DES (Dark Energy Survey) Year 3:** S8 ~ 0.77 +/- 0.02 (2022).
- **HSC (Hyper Suprime-Cam) Year 3:** S8 ~ 0.75 (May 2025) — consistent with KiDS/DES.

**Core Contradiction:** The same ΛCDM model that fits CMB data exceptionally well predicts ~8% more structure growth than is observed in the local universe. This is the mirror image of the Hubble tension: the H0 tension is about the expansion TIMELINE, while the S8 tension is about the CLUMPINESS of matter that expansion produces.

**Sub-propositions (≥3):**
1. P1 — Baryonic feedback effects on small scales (AGN feedback, supernova winds) may suppress the matter power spectrum more than current simulations predict. Attackable by comparing hydrodynamical simulations (IllustrisTNG, EAGLE, SIMBA) against weak lensing data.
2. P2 — Non-standard dark energy / modified gravity (e.g., running G, phantom dark energy, interacting dark sectors) can reduce S8 at late times. Attackable by fitting modified models to DESI BAO + lensing data.
3. P3 — Massive neutrinos suppress structure growth on small scales; does Σmν ~ 0.1-0.2 eV resolve the S8 tension without worsening H0?

**Journal Level:** **Physical Review D / MNRAS** — 2-3σ is below the 3σ threshold for "anomaly" status by some criteria. However, the persistence across three independent surveys (KiDS, DES, HSC) makes it credible. **Risk:** Below 3σ threshold; may be resolved by improved baryonic feedback modeling.

1. **L5-1 (Hubble Tension)** — STRONGEST: 5σ+, dozens of independent measurements, Decomposable into 5 sub-propositions, attackable with existing public data (Planck, SH0ES, H0DN, DESI, Pantheon+). Multi-field implications (cosmology + particle physics).

5. **L5-3 (Quantum Oscillations in Insulators)** — Narrow but deep anomaly, two materials only. The thermodynamic data from 2024 is very strong, but the number of independent materials is limited.
