# I. INTRODUCTION

The interpretation of hadron production yields in high-energy collisions rests on a deceptively simple assumption: that the hadronization process respects the symmetries of the underlying QCD Lagrangian. Among these, isospin symmetry—the near-degeneracy of up and down quarks, m_u ≈ m_d ≈ 3-5 MeV at the hadronic scale—is generally considered robust, with violations suppressed by (m_d-m_u)/Λ_QCD ~ 1%. When the NA61/SHINE Collaboration reported that the kaon yield ratio R_K ≡ [K⁺+K⁻]/[2K⁰_S] = 1.184 ± 0.061 in Ar+Sc collisions at √s_NN ≈ 5-17 GeV, deviating from the "QCD prediction" of R_K ≈ 1.03 by 4.7σ [1], it ignited a focused effort to understand whether isospin symmetry survives the transition from the QCD Lagrangian to non-perturbative hadronic observables.

The stakes are high. If R_K > 1 genuinely signals isospin violation in hadronization, it would demand a revision of the standard string-fragmentation picture in which u and d quark pairs are produced with equal probability from the QCD vacuum, P(u) = P(d). This assumption is baked into every major Monte Carlo generator—PYTHIA [2], HERWIG [3], SHERPA [4]—and every heavy-ion transport model—UrQMD [5], SMASH [6], GiBUU [7], PHSD [8]. A violation would ripple across the entire phenomenology of particle production.

The experimental landscape is now extensive. The ISO-BREAK 25 workshop [9] compiled world data spanning e⁺e⁻, pp, pA, and AA collisions from √s ≈ 3 GeV to 5 TeV, finding a persistent excess R_K ≈ 1.2. In e⁺e⁻ at BESIII (√s = 2.0-3.7 GeV), the K± cross sections are systematically higher than K⁰_S by a factor of approximately 1.4 [10]. In heavy-ion collisions at SPS energies, the signal is strongest; at RHIC and LHC energies, it weakens or vanishes. The pattern has motivated proposals ranging from medium-modified KNΛ/KNΣ couplings [11] to quark recombination in the hadronization boundary [12], to genuine isospin-breaking new physics.

In this paper, we perform a systematic reanalysis of the R_K anomaly across all available collision systems and energies. Our approach is organized in three layers, corresponding to the three distinct physical effects that, we argue, jointly account for the entirety of the observed R_K > 1:

**Layer 1 — e_q² charge weighting in e⁺e⁻ (Sec. II).** In e⁺e⁻ annihilation, the electromagnetic production vertices weight uū pairs four times more heavily than dd̄ pairs (∝ Q_q²). Combined with the leading-particle effect—the tendency of the initial quark to end up in a same-flavor hadron—this produces K⁺/K⁰ > 1 even when the fragmentation functions themselves respect isospin symmetry, P(u) = P(d). The leading-particle effect is diluted at higher √s by the increasing charged-particle multiplicity, producing an energy-dependent K±/K⁰_S that decreases from ≈1.4 at 3 GeV to ≈1.0 at 91 GeV. This energy dependence has been misinterpreted as evidence for an energy-dependent P(u)/P(d) [11, 13]; we show it is the expected behavior of e_q² charge weighting plus isospin-symmetric fragmentation.

**Layer 2 — Associated production asymmetry in heavy-ion collisions (Sec. III).** In heavy-ion collisions at SPS energies, a significant fraction of kaons originates from associated production, NN → N K Y (Y = Λ, Σ). The cross section for the Λ channel (producing K⁺) exceeds that for the Σ channel (producing K⁰) by a large factor—near threshold, Λ/Σ cross section ratios of ~9-28 have been measured by COSY-ANKE [14] and COSY-11 [15]; at high energies, the "bare" amplitude-squared ratio is ≈3.1 [16], with the near-threshold enhancement arising from strong Λp final-state interactions. We quantify the contribution of this asymmetry to R_K and find it sufficient to account for the entire residual excess in heavy-ion data after baseline correction.

**Layer 3 — Synthesis and falsifiable prediction (Sec. IV).** We propose a Double Ratio Observable (DRO) that cancels the dominant systematic uncertainties—detector acceptance, feed-down corrections, and e_q² weighting—providing a direct, model-independent probe of P(u)/P(d). If P(u)/P(d) = 1, as our analysis concludes, DRO = 1 in all collision systems at all energies. We estimate that existing data from NA61/SHINE, STAR, and ALICE can achieve a sensitivity of ±0.06, sufficient to exclude |P(u)/P(d) - 1| > 0.12 at 95% CL.

Our central conclusion is that R_K > 1 is not anomalous. Three well-established Standard Model effects—e_q² charge weighting, associated-production asymmetry, and φ → KK̄ branching ratios—jointly account for all available data from 3 GeV to 5 TeV, in e⁺e⁻ and heavy-ion collisions alike. The reported 4.7σ significance reduces to approximately 1-2σ after proper baseline correction and consideration of non-Gaussian ratio distributions. The R_K anomaly, which has motivated an active research program into isospin violation in hadronization, appears to be the expected behavior of known physics, consistently applied.
