# Abstract and Introduction — Autoregressive Transformer NQS Systematic Bias on Geometrically Frustrated Lattices

## Abstract

The ground state of the spin-1/2 Kagome Heisenberg antiferromagnet has remained a central unresolved problem in quantum magnetism for three decades, with two competing propositions: a gapless Dirac quantum spin liquid (QSL) supported by variational Monte Carlo calculations [1,2], and a gapped Z2 QSL favored by cylinder density-matrix renormalization group studies [3-5]. The systematic biases inherent to each method—VMC favoring gapless states and cylinder DMRG favoring gapped states [6]—have created an impasse that calls for an unbiased numerical arbiter. Autoregressive Transformer neural quantum states (NQS) with spatial attention [7] were recently proposed as such an arbiter. In this work, we analyze the suitability of this architecture for the Kagome problem and identify a systematic symmetry-breaking bias rooted in the autoregressive factorization itself. The snake-scan ordering employed to traverse the two-dimensional lattice explicitly breaks the Kagome D6 point-group symmetry, introducing an effective one-dimensional virtual boundary. This boundary generates a logarithmic free-energy penalty—scaling as ln L, where L is the linear system size—that is systematically larger for gapless states possessing long-range entanglement than for gapped states with short-range correlations [10]. The resulting variational bias toward gapped states cannot be removed by deeper networks, extended training, or hyperparameter tuning, since its origin lies in the factorization structure rather than in optimization. We conclude that autoregressive Transformer NQS is unsuitable as an unbiased arbiter for geometrically frustrated lattices where point-group symmetry is critical for ground-state classification. As a constructive alternative, we identify equivariant group-convolutional neural network (GCNN) architectures that rigorously preserve lattice symmetries [9] as the appropriate variational ansatz class for such systems.

## I. INTRODUCTION

The spin-1/2 Heisenberg antiferromagnet on the Kagome lattice is among the most intensely studied models in frustrated quantum magnetism. The Kagome lattice, a network of corner-sharing triangles, maximizes geometric frustration: the classical ground-state manifold is macroscopically degenerate—with an entropy per spin s_0 ~ 0.502 ln 2 that scales extensively with system size—and quantum fluctuations are expected to select a quantum spin liquid (QSL) ground state devoid of magnetic order down to zero temperature [1,2]. Identifying the precise nature of this QSL—whether it possesses a finite spin gap (gapped Z2 or chiral QSL) or supports gapless fractionalized excitations (U(1) Dirac QSL)—has been a defining open question for more than three decades [1-5]. The answer carries significance well beyond the specific model: the Kagome antiferromagnet serves as a prototypical platform for topological order, emergent gauge fields, and fractionalized quasiparticles. Its experimental realizations in herbertsmithite and related kagome materials [2] make the theoretical resolution directly relevant to spectroscopic and thermodynamic measurements. Moreover, the Kagome problem functions as a benchmark for numerical methods in strongly correlated systems: a method capable of reliably distinguishing gapless from gapped QSL phases on this lattice would establish its credibility across the broader field of frustrated quantum magnetism.

Despite sustained effort, the two leading numerical methodologies have reached contradictory conclusions. On one side, variational Monte Carlo (VMC) with Gutzwiller-projected parton wave functions consistently finds that a gapless U(1) Dirac QSL yields the lowest variational energy, predicting characteristic pinch-point singularities in the spin structure factor S(q) ~ 1/|q - q_D| near the Dirac points, and algebraic spin-spin correlations decaying as ~1/r^4 at long distances [1,2]. On the other side, density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG) studies on cylinder geometries report a finite spin gap of Delta_s ~ 0.05-0.10J that survives systematic extrapolation in cylinder width and bond dimension to the two-dimensional limit [3-5], consistent with a gapped Z2 or chiral QSL. A crucial methodological development came from Ferrari et al. [6], who demonstrated that cylinder geometry can systematically make gapless states "appear gapped"—the finite circumference enforces a non-zero momentum quantization that masks the gapless spectrum of the two-dimensional limit. This implies that the DMRG consensus may itself be an artifact of the cylindrical topology rather than a reliable determination of the thermodynamic ground state. Conversely, VMC with Gutzwiller projection is inherently anchored to a specific parton mean-field starting point and may systematically miss competing gapped phases not captured by the projective construction. Neither method provides an unbiased arbitration of the gapless-versus-gapped question. The energy differences between the leading QSL candidate states on Kagome are on the scale of 0.002-0.01J per site [2]—strikingly small, and comparable to the estimated magnitude of the systematic biases in each method. The field urgently requires a numerical approach that does not share these geometric or mean-field biases.

Neural quantum states (NQS) have emerged as a powerful new class of variational wave functions that are, in principle, free from the geometric constraints and mean-field assumptions of conventional methods. By parameterizing the wave function amplitude directly as a neural network and optimizing via variational Monte Carlo, NQS can represent quantum states of arbitrary complexity without being restricted to a specific lattice geometry or parton construction. In particular, Viteritti et al. recently introduced a spatial-attention Transformer NQS with autoregressive factorization [7] that achieves high variational accuracy on ordered magnetic systems (e.g., the triangular lattice Heisenberg antiferromagnet) while scaling efficiently to large two-dimensional clusters. The architecture's ability to capture long-range correlations through self-attention, combined with the O(N) sampling efficiency of autoregressive factorization, led to the natural proposal that Transformer NQS could serve as an unbiased numerical arbiter for the Kagome ground-state controversy—a genuinely new tool constrained neither to a cylindrical geometry nor to a specific parton mean-field theory. It is essential to note, however, that this architecture has been validated exclusively on magnetically ordered lattices [7] and on systems with weak or no geometric frustration, such as the Shastry-Sutherland model [8]. Its behavior on systems that host gapless QSL phases—precisely the target application for the Kagome problem—has remained entirely untested prior to this work.

This paper asks a sharply focused question: is the autoregressive Transformer NQS architecture suitable as an unbiased arbiter for the Kagome Heisenberg antiferromagnet ground state? We answer in the negative. Through a combination of theoretical analysis—independently verified by two complementary derivation paths whose conclusions converged—and supporting numerical evidence, we identify a systematic symmetry-breaking bias inherent to the autoregressive factorization structure. The root cause is the snake-scan spatial ordering used to traverse the two-dimensional lattice for autoregressive sampling. This one-dimensional scanning path explicitly breaks the Kagome D6 point-group symmetry by establishing a preferred direction and introducing an effective virtual boundary at each scan-line turn. Through the mechanism identified by Lu et al. [10], this virtual boundary incurs a ln L free-energy penalty originating from the mutual information bottleneck of the factorized conditional distribution. We show that this penalty is systematically larger for gapless quantum states—whose long-range entanglement structure is fundamentally incompatible with the one-dimensional causal ordering—than for gapped states whose correlations decay exponentially. The bias direction is therefore locked: autoregressive Transformer NQS will systematically favor gapped variational states regardless of the identity of the true ground state. Critically, this bias is not a training deficiency. It cannot be eliminated by increasing network depth, extending training duration, enlarging the data set, or tuning hyperparameters, because it originates in the factorization ansatz itself rather than in any aspect of the optimization procedure. The point is not that the Transformer NQS lacks the expressive capacity to represent gapless QSL wave functions—the question of representability is distinct from the question of learnability under variational energy minimization, as demonstrated by Passetti and Kennes [11] in the related context of initialization-dependent entanglement phase transitions. Rather, the architecture imposes a structural preference that distorts the effective energy landscape, making gapped states artificially favorable in the variational search. As a constructive counterpoint, we note that equivariant group-convolutional neural network (GCNN) architectures [9] rigorously preserve the full space-group symmetry of the Kagome lattice by construction, providing an appropriate alternative for geometrically frustrated systems where point-group symmetry is critical to ground-state classification. We emphasize that our negative conclusion is specific to the combination of autoregressive factorization and high geometric frustration [8]; we explicitly delineate the conditions under which autoregressive architectures remain viable.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section II presents the theoretical analysis of the symmetry-breaking mechanism, organized into four arguments: the explicit breaking of lattice point-group symmetry by the autoregressive scanning order (II A), the statistical-mechanical derivation of the virtual-boundary free-energy penalty and its logarithmic scaling (II B), the direction and irreparability of the resulting variational bias (II C), and the structural insufficiency of validation on weakly frustrated reference systems (II D). Section III provides numerical evidence from direct comparisons between autoregressive Transformer NQS and symmetry-preserving GCNN architectures on the Kagome lattice, including finite-size scaling of the energy gap. Section IV discusses the scope and limits of our negative result, reconciling it with the demonstrated success of Transformer NQS on the Shastry-Sutherland model [8] and other non-geometrically-frustrated systems, and formulates a quantitative criterion for determining when autoregressive versus equivariant NQS architectures should be employed. We conclude in Section V by summarizing the implications for the broader NQS methodology in frustrated magnetism and for the Kagome ground-state problem in particular.
