### Relation to Prior Work: The Entropy Arrow Distinction

The idea that information-theoretic constraints underlie physical law is not new. Wheeler's "it from bit" (1990), Landauer's principle (1961), and Verlinde's entropic gravity (2011) all explored this territory. More recently, a converged 2023–2025 literature has independently arrived at the proposition that information constraints may not merely describe the universe but determine it—that the universe had to be this way.

Among these, Vopson's "second law of infodynamics" (2023, 2024, 2025; published in AIP Advances) bears the closest structural resemblance to our framework and therefore requires the clearest differentiation. Vopson proposes that information entropy in isolated physical systems either remains constant or decreases over time—the opposite of thermodynamic entropy. From this principle, he derives Newtonian gravity as an emergent consequence of the universe's tendency to minimize information entropy: matter clumps because concentrated mass reduces the computational/informational burden on the underlying substrate. The framework further proposes a mass-energy-information equivalence principle, treating information as a conserved physical quantity alongside mass and energy.

Our framework and Vopson's share a common ancestor—the intuition that information is not merely a language for describing physics but its generative constraint—and a common ambition—to derive physical law from information-theoretic first principles rather than postulating it. But they differ in a way that is physically substantive, not semantic:

| Dimension | Vopson (2023–2025) | This Work |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Entropy arrow** | Information entropy **decreases** (or stays constant) in isolated systems | Shannon entropy **increases** as information flows from high-occupancy to low-occupancy cells |
| **Gravity** | Derived: gravity = the force that minimizes information entropy | Open problem O3: gravity from q-gradient not yet derived |
| **Axiom status** | Mass-energy-information equivalence is an input postulate | A1 and A2 are proven attractors, not axioms |
| **Time** | Not addressed | Derived: time = causal DAG of overflow events |
| **Classical/quantum** | Not addressed | Derived: classical = capacity exhaustion, quantum = finite-environment reversibility |
| **Falsifiable prediction** | Not yet formulated as a ratio test | τ ∝ ρ for equal-mass objects (opposite sign to Penrose/Diósi) |

The entropy arrow difference is the key physical discriminator. Vopson's infodynamics predicts that information spontaneously compresses—isolated systems shed information, becoming simpler over time. Our framework predicts the opposite: Shannon entropy increases as information flows from high-occupancy (low-q, classical) to low-occupancy (high-q, quantum) regions. Irreversibility is not the universe minimizing information but the universe running out of capacity to reverse.

These two arrows make different predictions in principle. If information entropy decreases, a closed quantum system should spontaneously lose superposition complexity over time even without environmental coupling—decoherence as information compression. If Shannon entropy increases via capacity exhaustion, the same system decoheres because its cells fill up, not because information is being compressed. The density-scaling prediction in Section V (τ ∝ ρ, opposite in sign to Penrose/Diósi) is one experimental lever for distinguishing these hypotheses, though the direct Vopson-vs-DGF comparison requires additional theoretical development on both sides.

The deeper question is whether these two arrows are truly contradictory or whether they describe different regimes of the same underlying information dynamics—analogous to how thermodynamic entropy can increase locally while gravitational entropy (the clumping of matter) produces the opposite trend. We do not resolve this question here. We note only that the frameworks differ in their present formulation, that the difference is physically meaningful, and that the convergence of multiple independent lines of inquiry on information-theoretic inevitability—Vopson's infodynamics, Carboni's informational inevitability (2025), Youvan's attractor invariants (2025), and the present work—suggests that this is a productive direction, not a crowded one.

Our contribution is not the general idea that information constraints shape physical law. It is the specific proof that A1 and A2 are attractors (not postulates), the derivation of time and the classical-quantum distinction from these attractors alone, and a parameter-free falsifiable prediction that distinguishes our framework from all existing models of intrinsic decoherence.
