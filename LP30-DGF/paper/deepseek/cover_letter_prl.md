Please consider the manuscript "Macrocosmic Decoherence as Directed Spatial-Information Overflow" for publication in Physical Review Letters.

The manuscript advances a strong but sharply formulated claim: macroscopic classicality is the irreversible overflow of spatial interference information into lower-mass receiver degrees of freedom.  The revised version supplies the missing mathematical links that such a claim requires.

First, the overflow variable \(q\) is given a microscopic definition as the fraction of occupied Planck cells whose branch-recombination visibility is inaccessible to the system's Lorentzian branch.  Second, the irreversibility is represented by a Fokker-Planck equation with entropy-gradient drift toward larger overflow, not by zero-mean noise alone.  Third, the projection angle \(\theta(q)=\pi q\) is derived from a two-sector information geometry rather than asserted as a Wick-rotation analogy.  Fourth, the low-mass receiver sector enters the decoherence kernel through a spectral density \(J_{\rm recv}(\omega)\), whose local Markovian limit reduces to the \(p^4/3m\) GUP kernel.

The resulting theorem chain links cell access weights, directed overflow (driven by the capacity gradient: low-mass = low-occupancy = high receiver capacity), Euler residual mass, the \(2m_p/\pi\) coherent-branch endpoint, and receiver-mediated decoherence.  A crucial distinction between locked and flowable information resolves the mass-decoherence paradox and gives a two-layer structure consistent with standard open quantum systems.
