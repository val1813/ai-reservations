\begin{letter}{Editor\\Physical Review Letters}

\opening{Dear Editor,}

We submit ``Capacity-Normalized Bound on Non-Markovian Backflow from Finite Information Capacity'' for consideration as a Letter in Physical Review Letters.

\textbf{What is new.} Megier, Smirne, and Vacchini [Phys. Rev. Lett. 127, 030401 (2021)] proved an entropic upper bound on non-Markovian backflow that is fully general (any CPTP dynamics) but requires quantum state tomography for evaluation. We derive a combinatorially simpler bound on the capacity-normalized backflow ratio $\mathcal{R} \equiv N_{\rm back}/C_F \leq q_S/q_E$. The bound depends only on projective pointer-basis population averages $q_S$, $q_E$, accessible without tomography. The proof uses the pigeonhole principle: each qubit toggles $|0\rangle \to |1\rangle$ at most once under unidirectional Lindblad dynamics.

\textbf{Why PRL.} The result complements the MSV entropic bound by providing an operationally accessible constraint. Where the MSV bound requires reconstructing the joint system-environment density matrix, our bound requires only single-qubit population measurements---a dramatic reduction in experimental overhead. The bound connects finite information capacity to the established theory of non-Markovianity, providing a new quantitative tool in the BLP--RHP--Buscemi landscape of detection, divisibility-based characterization, and causal classification.

\textbf{Relation to prior work.} The Breuer-Laine-Piilo measure detects non-Markovianity; Rivas, Huelga, and Plenio [PRL 105, 050403 (2010)] characterized it via CP-divisibility; Buscemi et al. classify revivals as causal or noncausal; MSV bound backflow entropically. Our contribution is a combinatorially-derived, operationally simple bound that is complementary to MSV---narrower in domain (unidirectional Lindblad channels) but requiring only projective measurements.

Suggested referees: Bassano Vacchini (non-Markovian dynamics, co-author of the MSV bound), \'A. Rivas (CP-divisibility framework), Dariusz Chru\'sci\'nski (open quantum systems), and Fabio Costa (quantum causality).
