## What we claim

We claim that the microscopic-mechanism interpretation of island information encoding is boundary-algebra dependent. In a post-Page SYK/JT code subspace, a narrow algebra of simple boundary observables can exhibit an O(1) relative-entropy gap with respect to an island excitation. By contrast, a wide algebra including the radiation/bath degrees of freedom needed for entanglement-wedge reconstruction has no independent O(1) gap once approximate Petz/QEC recovery is assumed; the remaining defect is controlled by the recovery error.

## What we do not claim

We do not claim that the island formula is wrong. We do not prove a full Hilbert-space theorem for canonical Type III_1 algebras. We do not compute the exact Jones/Kosaki-Longo index `[A_full:A_bdy]`. We do not show that Lorentzian transport is impossible. We also do not show that every algebraic recovery map is efficiently implementable.

## Scope and assumptions

The argument is a code-subspace diagnostic. It assumes a post-Page regime, a specified split between narrow and wide boundary algebras, and an approximate recovery map for the wide algebra. Microcanonical Type II_infinity crossed-product results are used only as controlled algebraic guidance, not as automatic replacements for canonical Type III_1 statements. The result should therefore be stated as a conceptual proposition rather than a theorem.
